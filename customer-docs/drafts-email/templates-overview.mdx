A template gives Ergo a pattern for an email. It helps draft quality. It does not replace your review.

## Who can use this

- Owners and team Admins who make templates for a team. Team templates are in **Settings → Email Drafting** (below **Team**) → **Post-call email templates**. Members of the team can read them but cannot change them.
- Each person with a Standard seat who wants personal templates (**Settings → Email Drafting → Templates → My Templates**).

## Before you start

- Decide which email the template covers.
- Collect example text that is safe to reuse.

## Template parts

- **Template Name**: the label that people see.
- **Template Description**: the situation in which Ergo must use the template, for example "Post-demo follow-up". Ergo uses it to select a template.
- **Template Content**: the email pattern.
- **[Brackets]**: text in square brackets tells Ergo what to write at that place, for example [next steps] or [key pain points]. There are no template variables to set up.
- **Attachments**: files that Ergo adds to each draft that it makes from the template (up to 10 files, 25 MB in total).

## What to expect

- There are two scopes: **My Templates** (personal, used in your drafts) and team templates (used for the post-call drafts of the team members). There is no organization scope.
- For a post-call draft, Ergo selects the template whose name and description fit the meeting, or no template. When it selects a template, it follows the template and fills the [brackets].
- To stop the use of templates in your drafts, turn off **Use templates in generated emails**.
- A template change affects future drafts. It does not change existing drafts.

## Common issues

- The description is too vague. Ergo uses the template in the wrong situation.
- The content has old or customer-specific details.
- You expect a template change to update drafts that exist.
